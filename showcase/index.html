Nine tools.
One company.

Glasshouse read half a million documents across Slack, Gmail, Jira, Confluence, GitHub, Drive, HubSpot, Linear and Fireflies — and found every place the company wrote the same thing down two different ways. Nobody had to ask.

A company keeps the same fact in nine places. Over time those facts drift apart, and nobody notices — because nobody is looking. You find out when somebody acts on the wrong one.

What it does

Three jobs, one graph

01

Know who everybody is

@jae, J. Okafor, jokafor@corp.com and a typo'd domain are one person. Resolving a name is a single hop through the graph.

163,262 merges refused by hard rules
02

Notice the contradictions

Confluence says Benji owns it. Slack says Liam. Those two claims are joined by a real edge, so "what do we contradict ourselves about" is one query — not a search.

816 contradiction edges · 75 supersessions
03

Pick a side, and say why

A reviewed page outranks a chat message. Newer outranks older. Two sources outrank one. It is arithmetic — no model is called, so the same claims settle the same way every time.

10 shuffled runs · byte-identical verdicts
The part that matters

trust is too close to choose: 0.958 against 0.900 is a gap of 0.058, and 0.06 is the least that would settle it

Forty-eight of a hundred and seventy-three end this way. Two sources, equally credible — and the truthful answer is that this company has not actually decided yet. It tells you why it can't, in a sentence you can check.

Built on HydraDB

Used twice, for two different jobs

Turn the engine off and every one of these questions returns zero. It is not storing a result computed somewhere else — it is where the reasoning happens.

A question that says "too many requests errors" still finds the page that only ever says 429. Keyword search cannot do that.

How a question finds its page

Three ways in. Two of them are hops.

Every question is run through all three at once, and the trace records which one actually found the document — so you can see exactly where the answer came from.

Measured, not estimated

Graded against a key
the system cannot see

The answer key lives outside the repository, and only the scoring script may read it. Every answer is checked one required fact at a time by a separate judge, after the answer is already final. Twenty questions per category, one run, 100 answers — the raw report is data/state/answer_grade.json in the repo.

# open http://127.0.0.1:8080
